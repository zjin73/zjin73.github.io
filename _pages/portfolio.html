RESEARCH / SELECTED EXPERIENCE

Models. Motion. Decisions.

My work starts with the dynamics of physical systems and extends toward the planning and coordination of autonomous agents.

Research experience

01 / PREDICTIVE CONTROL

Learning models for nonlinear control

My research experience includes Koopman-based representations of nonlinear dynamics and disturbance prediction for model predictive control. I am interested in how data-driven models can support useful predictions while retaining a structure suitable for optimization.

02 / MOTION PLANNING

Generative planning for robotic arms

I work on learning-based motion planning for manipulators, including diffusion-based trajectory generation and multi-arm settings. The central challenge is to connect flexible learned motion priors with collision avoidance, trajectory smoothness, and executable robot motion.

03 / COOPERATIVE ROBOTICS

Coordinating a multi-arm space robot

I have studied cooperative manipulation using multi-agent reinforcement learning in a simulated space robot with four UR5 arms. This work connects decentralized policy decisions with low-level joint tracking and evaluates how coordination behaves in the presence of disturbances.

Questions I want to explore

IDENTIFICATION & ACTION

When should an agent act to learn?

How can an autonomous system select actions that improve its model while making progress on a task? I am interested in information gathering, model uncertainty, and planning in belief space.

LEARNING & FEASIBILITY

How can learned plans respect physical constraints?

I would like to explore the combination of generative motion models with optimization-based control, using learned proposals to guide planning while explicitly checking feasibility.

AGENTS & SHARED RESOURCES

How can local decisions lead to reliable coordination?

I am interested in systems where agents have different observations but share constraints, including robotic teams and distributed energy or industrial systems.

These questions describe future research interests; they are distinct from the research experience summarized above.
